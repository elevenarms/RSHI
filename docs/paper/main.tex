\large\mdseries Lifting a weak open model at dishwasher loading with a self-rewriting team}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
How best to load a dishwasher is a combinatorial, physical problem with an unusually honest reward:
a simulated wash decides which items come out clean \citep{composo2026dishwasherbench}. We use it
as a testbed for self-improving multi-agent systems built only from open, inexpensive models. A
deliberately weak loader (Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B, thinking off) is surrounded by a planner,
a vision critic, a coach and a librarian, all open Nemotron models on Crusoe serverless inference.
Between generations the team rewrites a typed genome of its own prompts, playbook, tool policy and
structure, and a deterministic gatekeeper keeps an edit only if it beats the incumbent on paired,
fresh tasks. On a held-out exam whose levels contain item kinds and soils never seen in training,
the evolved team scores \ChampionExam{} against \BaselineExam{} for the bare loader
(\ExamDelta{}\,pp). We report what the loop changed, what it cost, and why the evidence is still thin.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recursive self-improvement is usually discussed for systems that rewrite their own weights. A
humbler, testable version keeps the model fixed and lets the system rewrite the \emph{scaffold}
around it: prompts, memory, tools and the division of labour
\citep{zelikman2023stop,hu2024adas,zhang2025dgm}. Two things make such loops hard to trust. The
reward is often an LLM judge, which can be flattered and drifts \citep{zheng2023judge}, and a loop
that optimises a proxy finds the proxy's seams
\citep{skalse2022rewardhacking,krakovna2020specgaming,manheim2018goodhart}. And improvements are
usually measured on the distribution they were selected on.

We take the problem of loading a dishwasher, using DishwasherBench, Composo's open (MIT) benchmark
\citep{composo2026dishwasherbench}. It models one real machine (Bosch SMV4HAX40G): items are placed
with deterministic rigid-body physics (Jolt) through 13 MCP tools, then a GPU wash simulation
(WebGPU via Dawn) of the Eco~50 programme with one detergent capsule, calibrated on washes of the
real machine (parameter set \texttt{anchored-v8.owner-capsule}), decides which items are clean
against a threshold derived from the IEC~60436 scale \citep{iec60436_2025,en60436_2020,eilts2023roundrobin}.
An item scores its points only if it comes out clean. The reward is \emph{objective} (points over
points available), \emph{deterministic} (a load always settles and washes the same way, and every
episode is verifiable by replay) and \emph{unfakeable}: a bowl mouth-up under a dead spot of the
spray arm stays dirty however eloquently the agent describes it. It is also hard: not everything
fits, so the player chooses what to load as well as how; jets reach only part of the load
\citep{perezmohedano2015pept,perezmohedano2017integrated}; and choosing a subset under capacity is
a knapsack over strongly NP-hard 3D packing \citep{kellerer2004knapsack,martello2000threedim}.
Household benchmarks score symbolic goal predicates
\citep{srivastava2021behavior,shridhar2020alfred,puig2018virtualhome,savva2019habitat}, and prior
dishwasher-loading work scores grasp success or agreement with a human preference
\citep{voysey2021dishwasher,jain2023transformers,kapelyukh2021myhouse,lin2021gnn}; to our knowledge
none scores whether the items come out clean, the gap DishwasherBench fills.

The benchmark, simulator, calibration, levels and harness are Composo's work
\citep{composo2026dishwasherbench}. Ours is the self-improving team (\emph{crew}), its memory,
Gatekeeper, exam protocol and visualiser, plus cosmetic water, watch and turntable changes to the
benchmark's web client that touch neither simulation nor scoring. We claim that a team of cheap
open models can lift a weak loader by rewriting the system around it, keeping only changes that
measurably help, and that the lift survives on unseen tasks. Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A team whose rewritable state is a typed, versioned \emph{genome}, edited only through six
typed operations, with the judging roles (memory accounting, acceptance) in deterministic code: the
team can change how it plays, never how it is graded.
\item A paired acceptance rule over shared seeds in a deterministic simulator, a three-layer memory
with shrunken Bayesian confidence, and a held-out exam on task families with new kinds and soils.
\item An end-to-end run on open Nemotron models with lineage, costs and limits reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Scaffold self-improvement.} STOP recursively improves the improver around a fixed LM
\citep{zelikman2023stop}; ADAS has a meta-agent program new agents \citep{hu2024adas}; the Darwin
G\"odel Machine keeps self-edits that validate, in an open-ended archive \citep{zhang2025dgm};
G\"odel Agent and SICA rewrite their own logic or code \citep{yin2024godel,robeyns2025sica}. AFlow
searches workflows with MCTS and lets cheap models match expensive ones \citep{zhang2024aflow};
GPTSwarm optimises prompts and agent connectivity \citep{zhuge2024gptswarm}; FunSearch and
AlphaEvolve gate LLM proposals with automatic evaluators
\citep{romeraparedes2024funsearch,novikov2025alphaevolve}. Our Coach is a typed, constrained
meta-agent in this line; we differ in the physical reward, the paired gate and held-out families.

\textbf{Prompt and program optimisation.} OPRO optimises from a scored history \citep{yang2023opro};
Promptbreeder and EvoPrompt evolve prompts by LLM mutation
\citep{fernando2023promptbreeder,guo2023evoprompt}; DSPy and MIPRO tune multi-stage LM programs
against a metric \citep{khattab2023dspy,opsahlong2024mipro}; TextGrad and Trace propagate textual
feedback \citep{yuksekgonul2024textgrad,cheng2024trace}; GEPA's reflection on traces is closest to
our Critic$\to$Coach loop \citep{agrawal2025gepa}. We widen the edit space to tools and team structure.

\textbf{Memory and multi-agent systems.} Reflexion stores verbal reflections \citep{shinn2023reflexion};
ExpeL and CLIN distil and transfer cross-task insights \citep{zhao2023expel,majumder2023clin};
Voyager and AWM grow verified skill or workflow libraries \citep{wang2023voyager,wang2024awm};
Generative Agents and MemGPT structure retrieval under a context budget
\citep{park2023generative,packer2023memgpt}. Unlike self-refinement \citep{madaan2023selfrefine},
our lessons are counted against physics. Role-based teams
\citep{wu2023autogen,hong2023metagpt,li2023camel,qian2023chatdev} and debate \citep{du2023debate}
follow fixed processes; ours edits its own structure. Voting and repeated sampling with a verifier
\citep{li2024moreagents,brown2024monkeys,snell2024ttc} trade compute for parameters; our simulator
is such a verifier, but far too slow to call per action.

\textbf{Search over loads.} Loading is constrained packing with a physical, black-box objective
\citep{wang2019stable,zhao2021online}; genetic algorithms \citep{holland1975adaptation,goldberg1989genetic},
CMA-ES over poses \citep{hansen2001cmaes}, quality-diversity \citep{mouret2015mapelites}, Bayesian
optimisation \citep{snoek2012practical} and MCTS over placement sequences
\citep{kocsis2006uct,browne2012mcts} are natural per-instance optimisers. We do not search loads
directly: every evaluation costs a wash, and the product is \emph{one load}, not a better loader,
so nothing transfers to a new set of items. We optimise the system, and test whether its
improvements carry to tasks they were not selected on. Search is a natural future tool for the team.

\section{Method}
\textbf{The team} (Table~\ref{tab:team}, Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}). The Loader plays through the
standard DishwasherBench harness tools \citep{composo2026dishwasherbench}, text only. Optionally a
Planner turns the level card and a memory brief into a per-item plan (rack, stance, what to leave
out). After each practice episode the Critic gets the item-by-item outcome (rack, stance, clean,
residue regions, retained water, movement), the plan, the playbook, the loader's last words and
two renders of the final load, and writes up to five tagged lessons with a polarity. The Coach
proposes edits; the Librarian condenses memory into a brief.

\textbf{Genome and typed edits.} A genome is an immutable record of everything the team may
rewrite: three prompts (Planner, loader preamble, Critic); a playbook of at most 25 tagged rules
with evidence links to lessons; team switches (\texttt{usePlanner}, \texttt{useMemoryBrief},
\texttt{preflightCheck}, which refuses \texttt{start\_cycle} before a read \texttt{check\_load}, and
the brief's lesson count); and loader settings (reasoning effort, images, denied tools, turn limit).
The Coach can change it only by \texttt{add\_rule}, \texttt{edit\_rule}, \texttt{remove\_rule},
\texttt{set\_prompt}, \texttt{set\_team} and \texttt{set\_loader}. A proposal carries a title and a
falsifiable hypothesis, should change one coherent thing so gains can be attributed, and is shown
past rejections. Invalid edits (ranges, sizes, tools that may never be denied) are dropped; a
child's id hashes its content with its parent, so the lineage is diffable. The baseline G0 is the
bare loader: no planner, no brief, no preflight, an empty playbook, 120 turns.

\textbf{Three-layer memory.} The \emph{episodic} layer keeps every training episode item by item;
the \emph{semantic} layer keeps lessons (from the Critic, and observed from placement statistics),
deduplicated on merge, with support $s$ and contradiction $c$ counts; the \emph{procedural} layer
keeps every genome and decision. Confidence is a Beta(1,1) posterior mean shrunk towards ignorance
while evidence is thin:
\begin{equation}
p=\tfrac{s+1}{n+2},\quad \kappa=\tfrac12+\big(p-\tfrac12\big)\tfrac{n}{n+K},\quad n=s+c,\ K=3,
\label{eq:conf}
\end{equation}
so one supporting episode gives $0.54$, five of five $0.72$, twenty of twenty $0.90$. A level's
brief lists smoothed clean rates $(k+1)/(m+2)$ of its kinds per rack and stance, then lessons with
$\kappa\ge0.5$ ranked by $\kappa\cdot\text{relevance}\cdot\log(1+n)$. Exam episodes are stored
apart and never read back.

\textbf{Gatekeeper.} Candidate $C$ and incumbent $I$ play the same fresh validation drills with the
same seeds. Let $P$ be the drills both played validly and $x_t$ the share on drill $t$; then
\begin{equation}
\bar\Delta=\tfrac{1}{|P|}\textstyle\sum_{t\in P}\big(x_t^{C}-x_t^{I}\big),\qquad
\text{accept}\iff \bar\Delta\ge\delta,
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
and the decision is void if $|P|<3$. Agent failures (turn or token limit, nothing loaded) score what
they scored; \emph{invalid} episodes (provider or harness errors) are replayed on the same task and
seed up to twice, and drills still unpaired are dropped for both genomes.\footnote{Our first
version counted provider errors as agent failures. In generation~2 the fast, non-thinking loader
at 8-way concurrency hit Crusoe's per-model request cap (HTTP 429), and both decisions were voided.
On cheap shared endpoints rate limits are a real systems constraint, and an acceptance rule must
not confuse infrastructure noise with policy quality.} Since loading and washing are deterministic,
pairing cancels drill difficulty, leaving the genome's effect plus sampling noise. The accepted
candidate with the largest $\bar\Delta$ becomes the incumbent; its validation episodes are the next
practice set.

\textbf{Exam: what ``held-out'' means.} Training and validation drills come from DishwasherBench
course levels 1--4 (tutorial, lunch for four, weeknight dinner, breakfast), the exam from levels
5--7 (mixed family, baking day, dinner party) \citep{composo2026dishwasherbench}. These are new task
families, not just new seeds: they hold kinds and soils training never showed (baby bottle and
teat, spouted beakers, lunch tubs, baking tray, tin, platter, board; burnt sugar, overnight soils).
G0 and the champion play identical exam drills; nothing is learned from them.

\section{Experimental Setup}
All models run on Crusoe Managed (serverless) Inference via an OpenAI-compatible API, with a
client-side limiter (600 requests per minute per model) and byte-stable prompts for prefix
caching (83--90\% of input tokens hit the cache in smoke runs). The loader is the weakest Nemotron
that still plays: 41--70\% in smokes, where Nemotron-3-Nano-Omni-Reasoning saturated at 100\% and
Nano-30B or Omni with thinking off looped on refused calls. A drill is 12 items of a
DishwasherBench level drawn by seed, cutlery in proportion. We ran \Generations{} generations:
\PracticeTasks{} baseline practice drills, \ProposalsPerGen{} proposals and \ValTasks{} paired
fresh validation drills per generation, $\delta=\Margin$, then an exam on \ExamTasks{} held-out
drills (Table~\ref{tab:exam}). Washes use the fast tier (4\,mm blocks rather than 2\,mm), whose
verdicts agree with the official tier's on 91\% of the standard load and 97\% of pieces on the
course levels \citep{composo2026dishwasherbench}.

The bare loader often wanders until the turn or token limit (about 115--120 turns, 3M input
tokens), sometimes loading nothing (0 of 87 points on one weeknight drill) or few items, yet what
it loads mostly comes out clean. It fails at planning and commitment, not physics, which suggests
where a team can help.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The design point we would defend is the division of authority: the models may propose anything the
edit grammar allows, but physics scores it and code decides, which is what makes a loop of cheap,
fallible models safe to leave running and what LLM-judged loops lack
\citep{zheng2023judge,amodei2016concrete}. The open question is credit assignment: does the lift
come from structure (a planner that commits the loader to a list) or from content (rules and
lessons about what washes clean)? The baseline's failure suggests structure; transfer to unseen
kinds would need some content.

\textbf{Limitations.} \emph{One machine}: one dishwasher, programme and detergent; the exam tests
transfer to new items, soils and loads, not new physics. \emph{Small $n$}: four validation and four
exam drills, one exam pass; no confidence interval worth the name, and a paired permutation or
sign test \citep{dror2018hitchhiker} on four drills has little power. \emph{Stochastic sampling}:
the simulator is deterministic but the models are not, so pairing removes task variance, not policy
variance. \emph{Fast tier}: a few percent of verdicts differ from the official tier.
\emph{Multiple comparisons}: \ProposalsPerGen{} candidates per generation are each tested at a fixed
margin and the best is kept, so validation gains are biased upwards, and repeated accept/reject on
one benchmark overfits it \citep{gelman2013forking,dwork2015reusable,benjamini1995fdr}; the held-out
exam, not the validation curve, is the evidence. \emph{Margin}: $\delta=\Margin$ over four drills is
one to two average items (about 70 points over 12 items) on a single drill, below the plausible
run-to-run noise of a sampling loader; a sequential test \citep{wald1945sequential} or repeated
validation would be the principled fix.

\section{Conclusion}
A team of open, inexpensive models can rewrite the system around a weak loader, keep only what the
physics confirms, and carry the improvement to tasks with objects it has never handled. The
dishwasher is a small domain, but its reward cannot be argued with, which is exactly what recursive
self-(home-)improvement needs. Next: more exam passes, an official-tier exam, and a search tool
(GA or MCTS over placements) that the team may decide to use.
